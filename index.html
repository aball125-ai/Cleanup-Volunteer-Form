Volunteer Registration

Community Cleanup

This form is for community members interested in volunteering at a local cleanup drive. The form will take about 3-5 minutes to fill out. This prototype does not process registrations or send confirmation emails.

F1 · Purpose and Expectations

Fields marked with an asterisk (*) are required. All other fields are optional.

This prototype does not collect or store personal information. Use fictional information when testing.

F4 · Requirements and Validation

Full name, email address, preferred shift, and cleaning area are mandatory. All other details are not mandatory.

Your email should be entered in a standard format, like name@example.com. The system will detect all missing mandatory details as well as validate the email address.

The full name should not exceed 80 characters, while additional information should not be more than 240 characters.

F3 · Volunteer Information

Enter your contact information. Fields marked with an asterisk (*) are required.

F3 · Volunteer Preferences

Choose your preferred shift, cleanup location, and activities. The remaining questions are optional.

F5 · State and Recovery Specimen

This part shows the use of the form in various states. Below are some examples that include an invalid email, error message, and disabled button. The form itself shows two states, namely the default and required states. The focus state on keyboard navigation will also be shown in the form.

  • Default: Fields appear empty and ready for entry.
  • Focus: The currently selected field has a visible focus indicator.
  • Required: Required fields are identified with an asterisk and checked before submission.
  • Valid: The valid email example shows an accepted email format.
  • Invalid: The invalid email example identifies the problem and explains how to correct it.
  • Disabled: The disabled button demonstrates a control that cannot currently be used.
  • Server-returned error: The static error example shows how an error would appear after a submission attempt.
